# Balance record

What simulated worlds measured about the rules, and the decisions taken from it. Measurements come from the simulation tools in `acceptance/` (`npm run coalitions`, `npm run sim`); scripted rulers are a rough stand-in for players, so every number here is to be checked again once real players exist.

## Coalitions

1. Coalitions of ten measured on 2026-10-31 (`npm run coalitions`, 150 turns, three groups of 5 economists, 3 warlords and 2 turtles, before garrisons existed): the coalition whose warlords strike together ended at 15,973 acres, an uncoordinated coalition at 14,473, ten unallied kingdoms at 13,929. Coordinated strikes win fewer but bigger battles (168 acres per victory against 93); being allied mostly helps by leaving fewer enemies. Both edges are real but modest.
1. Standing guard measured on 2026-10-31 (150 turns, Order's two turtles as guardians; run-to-run noise about 2%). Guards placed ahead of time did nothing: half the army every six turns left Order beaten 26 times instead of 15 because the guardians weakened themselves; a third, only when clearly stronger, was neutral (beaten 18 times, 1,700 acres lost against 1,595). Guards sent when an enemy army is seen marching on an ally cut Order's land lost by a third (beaten 12 times, 1,097 acres lost) and Order ended largest (16,045). Garrisons rarely turn a defeat into a win; they raise the defense the attacker must beat, so a won battle takes less land. Attackers scout and pick targets they can beat, so help is worth most once an army is on the way.
1. A fully coordinated coalition (guards sent on threat, and every warlord striking in the same attack turn through the war council) ended 150 turns at 19,393 acres against 14,335 for an uncoordinated coalition and 13,213 for ten unallied kingdoms (2026-10-31). Coordination is now the decisive edge. It is beaten more often (33 times, its armies are often away) but loses less land than either rival. Watch for runaway coalitions once real players exist; raid protection and the land capture ratio are the levers.
1. Allies pass gold through the market (decided 2026-10-31): a member offers a few pieces of goods at a dear price and an ally buys them. The market shows only the cheapest offers, so allies' offers are now always listed for their allies. The same trick lets a second account feed a main one, as it always could.
1. Funding measured on 2026-10-31 (`coalitions --guard --fund`, 150 turns; economists pay half their spare gold into warlords' asks). Against the same coalition unfunded (19,456 acres, beaten 30 times, 2,848 acres lost), the funded one ended at 20,663 acres and was beaten only 3 times, losing 249 acres: its warlords' armies doubled (677 to 1,430 soldiers each, 2,395 to 3,299 acres each), so rivals' spies found nothing worth attacking, while its economists grew 16% less land (1,855 to 1,556). The rivals, with no Order targets, fought less and ended with 7,500 soldiers instead of 5,000.
1. Soldiers are paid 1% of their training gold a turn (measured 2026-11-01, scripted rulers that train only what they can pay). Over 300 turns the three styles end within 12% of each other (economist 1,970 acres, warlord 2,186, turtle 2,215) against 22% unpaid (2,137, 1,888, 2,308): the warlord keeps 468 soldiers instead of 1,813, still wins 11 battles, and buys land with the rest; 90 of its soldiers deserted over the 300 turns. Coalition wars barely move: over 150 turns the coordinated coalition ends at 20,203 acres unfunded (19,456 unpaid) and 20,617 funded (20,663 unpaid), the rivals at 11,700 to 13,300. A funded coalition still ends 74% ahead of its rivals and is beaten 3 times in 150 turns.
1. Runaway coalitions checked 2026-11-01 (150 turns, guards and funding on). The coordinated coalition's lead is a skill gap, not a snowball: when Horde plays exactly as Order does (`coalitions --mirror`), the two end within 5% of each other (17,315 and 16,541 acres) and take little land from each other (2,612 and 1,327 acres in all), while the unallied ten fall behind (12,281). Its whole lead over weaker groups is captured land; both sides grow about the same by their own work. A won attack now takes less land from a smaller kingdom (a target half your size yields half the share): against weaker rivals the coordinated coalition ends at 19,887 acres instead of 20,617 (19,040 with the share falling with the square of the size ratio); between equals the gap narrows to 3% (16,644 and 16,208), the largest kingdom ends at 2,219 acres instead of 2,887, and the unallied ten keep 13,039 instead of 12,281. A coalition that plays better still wins clearly, which is the point of the game; what stops it is rivals who coordinate too.
1. Gold does not pile up, though the totals suggest it (checked 2026-10-31). Simulated economists end 150 turns holding about 8 million gold, but an acre then costs land²/10 gold, about 370,000 at 1,900 acres, and they earn about 3.2 million a turn, nearly all of which goes into land; the treasury is two or three turns of income that the scripted rulers keep back because they spend at most half of it per turn. Quadratic land prices are the sink, and gold held is plundered (up to 20% a raid), which is a second one. A rich funded warlord (16.8 million at turn 150, 3,355 acres, about 1.1 million an acre) shows the same: its barracks are full and land is dear, not idle.
1. Allies land together by joining a strike (decided 2026-11-02): the joining army waits at the border, at most four of its owner's turns, and fights right after the lead army lands, so allies need not pass turns in step.
1. Joining measured on 2026-11-02 (`coalitions --uneven --guard`, 120 turns, rulers playing in bursts of one to three turns at different ticks; two runs each, before the four-turn wait limit). Joining makes no measurable difference for scripted rulers: Order ended at 13,854 and 16,306 acres with joins, 15,043 and 14,419 marching alone; run-to-run noise at uneven pace is about 15%, against about 2% in lockstep. Scripted defenders do not react between hits, so landing together buys them nothing; the rule is for players who do. Uneven pace erodes the coordinated coalition's lead itself: in lockstep it ended near 19,500 acres against rivals near 13,000, at uneven pace near 15,000 against 12,000 to 15,000. Coordination is worth most when players play at the same time, which real coalitions will have to arrange.

## Kingdoms

1. Building points come in abundance (a new kingdom gets 100,000 a turn for 41 free acres); their lasting use is the great works. Measured on 2026-10-31: at the old prices every simulated ruler maxed all five great works within 120 turns of a 4,320-turn round, so the prices are now ten times higher and the works stay a goal for most of a round. Cutting builder output alone (to a tenth or a fifth) changed little.
1. A garrison of bowmen alone is a trap since bowmen strike nobody in melee: a raider lost only its arrow casualties while killing up to 94 bowmen per charge and broke through within a few charges. The rule sits in the Army page's tooltip (the steward only warns of what will not wait), and the simulated turtle mixes in infantry.
1. Protection after a defeat lasts 24 turns. Measured on 2026-10-31 in a 60-turn world: the economist was raided 2 times instead of 7 and the turtle once instead of 5, while the warlord ended at 461 acres instead of 688 and the raider at 183 instead of 330. Tune the length once real players exist.
1. The small kingdoms' production bonus (`common.smallKingdomProductionBonus`, five times the production at 100 built acres, fading to none at 1,000) measured on 2026-11-06 over 660 turns of one round (`sim --players economist,warlord,turtle,hoarder,economist@220,economist@440`), at 4 (as it is), 2 and 0. Final standings of the founders barely move: they end at 3,300 to 4,200 acres under every setting, though without the bonus the warlord leads (4,171) and with it the builders do (turtle 4,095). Staying small does not pay: a hoarder that keeps its 59 starting buildings for half the round piles up 83 million gold at 4 (17 million at 0), buys about 1,260 acres at once and then lacks the building points to fill them; it ends at 3,428 acres, below the founding economist (4,039) and just below one who joined at turn 220 (3,569), and at 3,395 without the bonus. The cubic price of land is what defuses it. The bonus is a newcomers' catch-up: two hundred and twenty turns after joining at turn 440 an economist holds 55% of the leader's land at 4, 54% at 2 and 44% at 0, and at thirty turns old it has 767 acres at 4, 634 at 2 and 378 at 0. Halving it costs newcomers almost nothing and halves the early flood of gold; the bonus is kept at 4 until real players say whether the first hours feel too generous.
1. One great work of each kind (decided 2026-11-06): production bonuses no longer pile up, since five gold shafts at level 5 made six times the gold and five at level 1 doubled it for a sixth of the price of one shaft raised to 5. Warehouses alone may be raised more than once, as their shields add up to a cap (75%) one warehouse cannot reach; merchant bots raise the one work of their trade instead of five. Over the same round (660 turns, bonus 4) the founders end about a tenth smaller (economist 3,652 acres instead of 4,039, turtle 3,651 instead of 4,095, warlord 3,087 instead of 3,588) while newcomers barely move (2,289 acres for one joining at turn 440, against 2,270), so a newcomer ends at 63% of the leader instead of 55%. The warlord, now the smallest founder, spends every coin on its army; watch it once real players exist.
1. Self-sufficient coalitions measured on 2026-11-06 (`npm run economy`, 120 turns, two coalitions each of four gold producers, a farmer, two blacksmiths and an iron miner, every member buying from allies first). The market stays inside the coalitions: they bought 96 to 100% of their pieces from their own members, and the merchant bots sold about 3% of all pieces traded. Bots raising one great work of their trade instead of five changed nothing measurable (2.7% of pieces against 2.2%, every kingdom's land within noise). The crafts do not pay alike: at the bots' prices (food 15, iron 25, tools 60) a worker earns 1,000 gold mining gold, 1,500 farming, 250 mining iron and about 370 at the forge, and after 120 turns the farmer holds 79% of a gold producer's land, the iron miner 37% and a blacksmith 28%. Iron responds to price (at 100 a piece the miner reaches 50 to 59%), tools do not: without warriors tools are only needed to train workers, so one blacksmith fills that market and a second one sells almost nothing and stalls near 500 acres; with a single blacksmith it still ends at 36%. The bots' prices now live in `bots.prices` and the blacksmith bot asks a fixed price like the others instead of following recent trades from a start of 140. The prices were raised the same day, after measuring coalitions of different make-ups (next entry).
1. The merchants' prices are now iron 100, tools 200 and weapons 200, food 15 (decided 2026-11-06), so that at the merchants' prices every worker earns what a gold miner does: an iron miner 10 × 100 = 1,000 gold a turn, a blacksmith 10 × (200 − 100) = 1,000, a gold miner 1,000 (a farmer 1,500). At the old prices (iron 25, tools and weapons 60) an iron miner earned a quarter of that, and with iron at 100 a blacksmith would lose gold on every piece it makes. Measured over 120 turns with `npm run economy` (two coalitions of eight, two runs each, which agreed within 2%; the coalition acting first in each tick ends 2 to 6% ahead of an identical rival, which is the noise floor), across iron/tools prices from 25/60 to 150/300:
   - Coalitions keep 90 to 100% of their purchases among their own members; the merchant bots sell 2 to 5% of all pieces.
   - Leaving out the iron miner (five gold producers instead of four) and buying iron on the market pays 10 to 12% more land when iron is at 25 to 50, comes out even at 75, and loses 2 to 3% at 100 and above. Making a good yourself pays exactly when the market's price for it is high, which is the bet each coalition takes.
   - A rival that corners iron (a member buying every piece outsiders and merchants offer, and its iron miner asking the same twice-the-merchants' price) cuts the iron-less coalition to 95 to 98% of its land at iron 75 and above; at 25 it cannot, as twice a cheap price is still cheap. The cornerer itself ends as large as a gold producer.
   - The iron miner ends at 58 to 96% of a gold producer's land at iron 100 to 150, against 39% at 25.
   - Blacksmiths end at 36 to 60% at every price: without warriors tools are only bought to train workers, so the market is small and a coalition with one blacksmith and an extra gold producer ends about 9% larger than one with two. Weapons are their other market; measure again once warriors are simulated.
   - Rulers who wait for a single farmer starve: one farmer's fields fall behind seven growing neighbours, and the last to buy each turn went hungry until its production collapsed. The simulated rulers now grow their own food when they hold less than two turns' worth, so the farmer sells what it can and the rest is grown at home.
   - With warriors in the coalitions (measured the same day, next point) blacksmiths find their market in weapons.
   Scripted rulers that make their own tools and weapons end within noise of the old prices: over 300 turns (`npm run sim`) their armies are the same size, and over 150 turns of coalition war (`coalitions --guard --fund`) the coordinated coalition ends near 19,850 acres at either price, beaten 3 or 12 times in different runs at both.

1. Cornering with carriers measured on 2026-11-06 (`npm run economy`, 120 turns, iron 100, tools and weapons 200, five runs per match, shuffled order). The cornering coalition now asks the doubled price only on the market and passes its iron to its own smiths by carriers: members write down what they lack, the cornerer and the iron miner hire the carriers that calls for and ship it, and bill each ally at the merchants' price through a one-piece offer priced at the debt, which the ally buys, the way allies pass gold through the market. Before the bills the iron miner gave its iron away and starved.
   - Leaving out the iron miner is a fair bet at these prices: an iron-less coalition ends even with a self-sufficient one (102% of its land, matches split).
   - Cornering does not choke a rival while the merchants restock: the iron-less rival still bought 145,000 to 235,000 iron from the merchant bots over the run and its smiths ended at 60% of a gold producer's land, the same as the cornering coalition's. The merchants' iron (the iron-miner bot offers up to 5,000 pieces at a time at 100, `bots.maxOffer.iron`) is the backstop that makes the choke fail; limiting it is the lever if cornering should bite.
   - Cornering is costly to the cornerer: it ends at 39 to 45% of a gold producer's land, as it buys the merchants' iron at up to twice their price and passes it on at their price; the coalition's iron miner ends at 37 to 63% (its carriers and markets take people and land), and in one run of five it starved for lack of gold before the bills came in.
   - The cornering coalition won four of five matches against the iron-less rival and five of five against a self-sufficient one, each time through its warrior (155 to 158% of a gold producer's land against 73 to 108%). Why its warrior wins is not explained by the iron, so read this as unexplained, not as cornering paying off.
