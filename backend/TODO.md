# What is left

## Coalitions

1. Coalitions of ten measured on 2026-10-31 (`npm run coalitions`, 150 turns, three groups of 5 economists, 3 warlords and 2 turtles, before garrisons existed): the coalition whose warlords strike together ended at 15,973 acres, an uncoordinated coalition at 14,473, ten unallied kingdoms at 13,929. Coordinated strikes win fewer but bigger battles (168 acres per victory against 93); being allied mostly helps by leaving fewer enemies. Both edges are real but modest.
1. Standing guard measured on 2026-10-31 (150 turns, Order's two turtles as guardians; run-to-run noise about 2%). Guards placed ahead of time did nothing: half the army every six turns left Order beaten 26 times instead of 15 because the guardians weakened themselves; a third, only when clearly stronger, was neutral (beaten 18 times, 1,700 acres lost against 1,595). Guards sent when an enemy army is seen marching on an ally cut Order's land lost by a third (beaten 12 times, 1,097 acres lost) and Order ended largest (16,045). Garrisons rarely turn a defeat into a win; they raise the defense the attacker must beat, so a won battle takes less land. Attackers scout and pick targets they can beat, so help is worth most once an army is on the way.
1. A fully coordinated coalition (guards sent on threat, and every warlord striking in the same attack turn through the war council) ended 150 turns at 19,393 acres against 14,335 for an uncoordinated coalition and 13,213 for ten unallied kingdoms (2026-10-31). Coordination is now the decisive edge. It is beaten more often (33 times, its armies are often away) but loses less land than either rival. Watch for runaway coalitions once real players exist; raid protection and the land capture ratio are the levers.
1. Allies pass gold through the market (decided 2026-10-31): a member offers a few pieces of goods at a dear price and an ally buys them. The market shows only the cheapest offers, so allies' offers are now always listed for their allies. The same trick lets a second account feed a main one, as it always could.
1. Funding measured on 2026-10-31 (`coalitions --guard --fund`, 150 turns; economists pay half their spare gold into warlords' asks). Against the same coalition unfunded (19,456 acres, beaten 30 times, 2,848 acres lost), the funded one ended at 20,663 acres and was beaten only 3 times, losing 249 acres: its warlords' armies doubled (677 to 1,430 soldiers each, 2,395 to 3,299 acres each), so rivals' spies found nothing worth attacking, while its economists grew 16% less land (1,855 to 1,556). The rivals, with no Order targets, fought less and ended with 7,500 soldiers instead of 5,000.
1. Soldiers are paid 1% of their training gold a turn (measured 2026-11-01, scripted rulers that train only what they can pay). Over 300 turns the three styles end within 12% of each other (economist 1,970 acres, warlord 2,186, turtle 2,215) against 22% unpaid (2,137, 1,888, 2,308): the warlord keeps 468 soldiers instead of 1,813, still wins 11 battles, and buys land with the rest; 90 of its soldiers deserted over the 300 turns. Coalition wars barely move: over 150 turns the coordinated coalition ends at 20,203 acres unfunded (19,456 unpaid) and 20,617 funded (20,663 unpaid), the rivals at 11,700 to 13,300. A funded coalition still ends 74% ahead of its rivals and is beaten 3 times in 150 turns.
1. Runaway coalitions checked 2026-11-01 (150 turns, guards and funding on). The coordinated coalition's lead is a skill gap, not a snowball: when Horde plays exactly as Order does (`coalitions --mirror`), the two end within 5% of each other (17,315 and 16,541 acres) and take little land from each other (2,612 and 1,327 acres in all), while the unallied ten fall behind (12,281). Its whole lead over weaker groups is captured land; both sides grow about the same by their own work. A won attack now takes less land from a smaller kingdom (a target half your size yields half the share): against weaker rivals the coordinated coalition ends at 19,887 acres instead of 20,617 (19,040 with the share falling with the square of the size ratio); between equals the gap narrows to 3% (16,644 and 16,208), the largest kingdom ends at 2,219 acres instead of 2,887, and the unallied ten keep 13,039 instead of 12,281. A coalition that plays better still wins clearly, which is the point of the game; what stops it is rivals who coordinate too.
1. Gold does not pile up, though the totals suggest it (checked 2026-10-31). Simulated economists end 150 turns holding about 8 million gold, but an acre then costs land²/10 gold, about 370,000 at 1,900 acres, and they earn about 3.2 million a turn, nearly all of which goes into land; the treasury is two or three turns of income that the scripted rulers keep back because they spend at most half of it per turn. Quadratic land prices are the sink, and gold held is plundered (up to 20% a raid), which is a second one. A rich funded warlord (16.8 million at turn 150, 3,355 acres, about 1.1 million an acre) shows the same: its barracks are full and land is dear, not idle.
1. Allies land together by joining a strike (decided 2026-11-02): the joining army waits at the border, at most four of its owner's turns, and fights right after the lead army lands, so allies need not pass turns in step.
1. Joining measured on 2026-11-02 (`coalitions --uneven --guard`, 120 turns, rulers playing in bursts of one to three turns at different ticks; two runs each, before the four-turn wait limit). Joining makes no measurable difference for scripted rulers: Order ended at 13,854 and 16,306 acres with joins, 15,043 and 14,419 marching alone; run-to-run noise at uneven pace is about 15%, against about 2% in lockstep. Scripted defenders do not react between hits, so landing together buys them nothing; the rule is for players who do. Uneven pace erodes the coordinated coalition's lead itself: in lockstep it ended near 19,500 acres against rivals near 13,000, at uneven pace near 15,000 against 12,000 to 15,000. Coordination is worth most when players play at the same time, which real coalitions will have to arrange.

## Game

1. Building points come in abundance (a new kingdom gets 100,000 a turn for 41 free acres); their lasting use is the great works. Measured on 2026-10-31: at the old prices every simulated ruler maxed all five great works within 120 turns of a 4,320-turn round, so the prices are now ten times higher and the works stay a goal for most of a round. Cutting builder output alone (to a tenth or a fifth) changed little.
1. A garrison of bowmen alone is a trap since bowmen strike nobody in melee: a raider lost only its arrow casualties while killing up to 94 bowmen per charge and broke through within a few charges. The rule sits in the Army page's tooltip (the steward only warns of what will not wait), and the simulated turtle mixes in infantry.
1. Protection after a defeat lasts 24 turns. Measured on 2026-10-31 in a 60-turn world: the economist was raided 2 times instead of 7 and the turtle once instead of 5, while the warlord ended at 461 acres instead of 688 and the raider at 183 instead of 330. Tune the length once real players exist.
1. Balance over 300 simulated turns (2026-10-31): before today economy with defense won clearly (turtle 1,833 acres, economist 1,690, warlord 1,453). With 15% land capture, raid protection, random events, great works at ten times the price and rulers that raise them, the three styles end within 16% of each other: turtle 2,332, economist 2,120, warlord 2,010. Scripted rulers are a rough stand-in; check again once real players exist.
